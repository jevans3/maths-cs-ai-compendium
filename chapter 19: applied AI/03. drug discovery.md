# Drug Discovery

*AI is transforming drug discovery by accelerating every stage of the pipeline, from target identification and hit finding to lead optimisation and clinical trial design, compressing timelines from decades to years.*

- Traditional drug discovery costs over $2B and takes 10-15 years to bring a single drug to market. AI does not eliminate biology — it eliminates the combinatorial search bottleneck. The chemical space of drug-like molecules is estimated at $10^{60}$; AI learns to navigate it efficiently rather than enumerating it.

## Molecular Representations

- Molecules must be encoded as numerical inputs before any ML model can process them:

    - **SMILES strings**: line notation representing molecular structure as text (e.g., `CCO` for ethanol). Can be fed directly into language models or tokenised and processed like sequences.

    - **Molecular graphs**: atoms as nodes, bonds as edges. Each node has features (atom type, charge, hybridisation); each edge has features (bond order, aromaticity). Processed by graph neural networks (GNNs).

    - **3D conformers**: the actual spatial arrangement of atoms (Cartesian coordinates). Critical for binding prediction since drugs bind to protein pockets in 3D space. Generated by tools such as RDKit and ETKDG.

    - **Fingerprints**: fixed-length binary vectors encoding the presence or absence of substructural patterns (Morgan/ECFP fingerprints). Fast and compact; used as baselines and in similarity search.

## Target Identification and Validation

- **Target identification**: determining which protein (or RNA, pathway, or mechanism) to modulate to treat a disease. ML approaches:

    - **Proteomics data analysis**: compare protein expression profiles between diseased and healthy tissue; use differential expression and network analysis to find dysregulated proteins.

    - **Knowledge graphs**: large biomedical graphs (Hetionet, OpenTargets) link genes, diseases, drugs, and pathways. Link prediction models suggest novel gene-disease associations.

    - **Phenotypic screens**: high-content imaging of cell populations treated with perturbations; foundation models (e.g., morphological profiling with Cell Painting) cluster perturbations by mechanism of action.

- **Target validation**: confirming the target is causal for disease and druggable. AlphaFold structures (chapter 19.02) inform whether the target has a tractable binding site.

## Virtual Screening

- **Virtual screening** ranks a large library of molecules by their predicted activity against a target before synthesising and testing any of them:

    - **Docking**: computationally place a small molecule into a protein binding pocket and estimate binding energy. Classical force-field docking (AutoDock Vina, Glide) is fast but approximate. DiffDock and related diffusion models predict binding poses more accurately.

    - **Structure-based virtual screening**: use the 3D structure of the target (from X-ray, cryo-EM, or AlphaFold) to dock and score millions of compounds. Scales to $10^9$ compounds with deep learning scoring functions (EquiBind, Uni-Mol).

    - **Ligand-based virtual screening**: when no target structure is available, search for molecules similar to known actives using fingerprint similarity (Tanimoto) or ML similarity models trained on bioactivity data.

## Molecular Property Prediction

- Given a molecular structure, predict a property without synthesising the molecule:

    - **ADMET properties**: Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, Toxicity. Models trained on experimental datasets (ChEMBL, ToxCast) predict whether a molecule is likely to be orally bioavailable, metabolically stable, or toxic.

    - **Binding affinity**: predict $K_d$, $K_i$, or $IC_{50}$ for a given target-ligand pair. Graph neural networks trained on bioactivity databases learn structure-activity relationships. Message passing captures local chemical environments.

    - **Quantum chemical properties**: predict molecular properties that normally require DFT calculations (HOMO-LUMO gap, dipole moment, reaction barriers). SchNet, DimeNet, and PaiNN use equivariant GNNs over 3D molecular graphs.

## Generative Molecular Design

- Instead of screening existing molecules, generate novel ones optimised for desired properties:

    - **Variational autoencoders (VAEs)**: encode molecules into a continuous latent space; decode back to molecules. Optimise in latent space using gradient-based methods or Bayesian optimisation. JTVAE encodes junction trees for validity guarantees.

    - **Generative adversarial networks (GANs)**: MolGAN generates molecular graphs directly. Mode collapse is a practical issue.

    - **Diffusion models**: DDPM and flow-matching over molecular graphs or 3D atomic coordinates. EDM (E(3) Equivariant Diffusion Model) generates 3D molecules; GeoDiff conditions generation on protein binding pockets.

    - **Language models over SMILES**: fine-tuned GPT-style models generate novel SMILES strings. Works well in practice due to the regularity of SMILES grammar; REINVENT uses RL with a diversity reward to push away from training data.

## Retrosynthesis and Synthesis Planning

- A promising molecule is useless if it cannot be synthesised. Retrosynthesis prediction frames synthesis planning as a tree search:

    - **Template-based**: classify the target molecule into reaction templates extracted from patent databases; apply templates to generate precursors. Fast and interpretable; limited by template coverage.

    - **Template-free**: predict disconnections using sequence-to-sequence models (target SMILES → precursor SMILES). MEGAN and Chemformer treat retrosynthesis as a translation problem.

    - **Monte Carlo Tree Search**: combine a retrosynthesis model with a tree search to find multi-step synthesis routes. AiZynthFinder and ASKCOS integrate with commercially available building block databases to find purchasable routes.

## Clinical Trial Design and Drug Repurposing

- **Drug repurposing**: identify new indications for approved drugs. Faster and cheaper than de-novo discovery since safety is already established. Graph-based methods mine drug-gene-disease networks; embedding similarity identifies drugs with similar mechanism profiles to a known efficacious drug.

- **Patient stratification**: use genomic and clinical data to identify which patients will respond to a given treatment. Reduces the effective trial size and increases success probability. Foundation models trained on electronic health records (EHR) predict trial eligibility and response.

- **Trial simulation**: digital twins and PK/PD models simulate drug behaviour in virtual patient populations, informing dosing and trial design before the first patient is enrolled.
