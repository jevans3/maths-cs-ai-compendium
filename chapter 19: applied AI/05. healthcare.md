# Healthcare AI

*AI in healthcare promises to improve diagnostic accuracy, accelerate clinical workflows, personalise treatment, and reduce costs — but it must meet a higher bar for safety, interpretability, and fairness than almost any other application domain.*

- A misclassified cat image is harmless. A missed tumour on a radiology scan, an incorrect drug interaction prediction, or a biased risk score that denies care to a marginalised patient can cost lives. Healthcare AI inherits every challenge of general ML and then adds regulatory approval, clinical workflow integration, and medical liability on top.

## Medical Imaging

- Radiology and pathology generate the most mature AI applications in medicine because images are structured, labels are available from historical reports, and the task (detect/classify/segment an abnormality) maps naturally to computer vision.

    - **Chest X-ray analysis**: CheXNet (Rajpurkar et al. 2017) matched radiologist-level performance on pneumonia detection. Modern systems (CheXpert, BioViL-T) use vision-language pretraining to generate free-text radiology reports from chest X-rays.

    - **Pathology (digital histology)**: whole-slide images (WSIs) are gigapixel images scanned from tissue sections. Multiple instance learning (MIL) aggregates patch-level predictions into slide-level diagnoses. CONCH and UNI are pathology foundation models pretrained on millions of WSIs.

    - **Segmentation**: nnU-Net automatically configures a 3D U-Net for any medical segmentation task (brain tumour, organ, lesion). Segment Anything Model (SAM) adapted for medical imaging (MedSAM) enables interactive segmentation with point prompts.

    - **Ophthalmology**: diabetic retinopathy grading from fundus photographs was one of the first FDA-cleared AI diagnostic devices (IDx-DR). DeepMind's model identifies over 50 retinal conditions and predicts cardiovascular risk from eye scans.

    - **Dermatology**: skin lesion classification at dermatologist level. Distribution shift is a known issue — models trained on light-skinned patients perform poorly on darker skin tones, reflecting dataset bias.

- **3D modalities**: CT and MRI add a spatial dimension. nnDetection handles 3D object detection. Foundation models such as UniverSeg perform few-shot segmentation across arbitrary anatomies without retraining.

## Clinical NLP and EHR Analysis

- Most clinical knowledge lives in unstructured free text: physician notes, discharge summaries, radiology reports, and pathology findings. Clinical NLP extracts structured information from this text.

    - **Named entity recognition (NER)**: extract medical entities (diseases, medications, procedures, dosages) from clinical text. Medical-specific models (spaCy's `en_core_sci_md`, scispaCy) outperform general NER models due to domain vocabulary.

    - **Relation extraction**: identify relationships between entities — "patient takes metformin for type 2 diabetes" — to populate structured knowledge graphs.

    - **Clinical BERT variants**: BioBERT, ClinicalBERT, and PubMedBERT are pretrained on medical corpora. They outperform BERT on clinical NLP benchmarks because medical language differs significantly from web text (abbreviations, negation patterns, Latin terms).

    - **ICD coding**: assign International Classification of Diseases codes to clinical notes for billing and epidemiological tracking. Transformer models match and often exceed human coders on established benchmarks.

    - **De-identification**: remove protected health information (PHI) — names, dates, addresses, IDs — to enable research use of clinical data under HIPAA. Rule-based and neural NER systems are combined for high recall.

- **EHR foundation models**: large models pretrained on longitudinal EHR data (diagnoses, medications, labs, procedures over time). NYUTron predicts 30-day readmission from clinical notes. ETHOS and Med-PaLM 2 handle open-ended clinical question answering.

## Drug Safety and Pharmacovigilance

- **Adverse drug event (ADE) detection**: extract reports of unexpected drug side effects from clinical notes, social media, and pharmacovigilance databases (FAERS). NLP models flag signals that human reviewers would miss in the volume of incoming reports.

- **Drug-drug interaction (DDI) prediction**: predict which drug pairs interact adversely. Graph models over drug-target-pathway networks predict novel interactions before they are discovered in clinical use.

- **Medication error prevention**: NLP in clinical decision support systems alerts prescribers to contraindicated combinations, incorrect doses, and allergy conflicts at the point of ordering.

## Health Monitoring and Wearables

- Wearable and ambient sensors generate continuous physiological signals that enable longitudinal health monitoring outside clinical settings.

    - **ECG analysis**: consumer wearables (Apple Watch, Fitbit) detect atrial fibrillation in real time. Deep learning models (e.g., from Hannun et al. 2019) classify 12-lead ECG arrhythmias at cardiologist level.

    - **Continuous glucose monitoring**: predict glucose trajectories in diabetic patients; ML models warn of impending hypoglycaemia 30–60 minutes ahead.

    - **Mental health**: passive sensing from smartphones (call logs, GPS, app usage, accelerometer) predicts depressive episodes. Models must be calibrated carefully for personalisation across diverse individuals.

    - **Remote patient monitoring**: ICU early-warning systems (sepsis risk scores like MIMIC-based LGBM models) predict clinical deterioration hours before clinical signs appear, enabling early intervention.

## Fairness, Explainability, and Regulation

- Healthcare AI faces unique ethical and regulatory constraints:

    - **Algorithmic bias**: models trained on historically biased datasets reproduce and amplify those biases. Pulse oximetry algorithms perform less accurately on darker skin tones; commercial sepsis predictors perform differently across racial groups. Audit datasets for demographic representation; report disaggregated performance.

    - **Explainability**: clinicians need to understand why a model made a recommendation. SHAP, integrated gradients, and attention visualisations provide feature attributions. Concept-based explanations (TCAV) map model internals to human-interpretable medical concepts.

    - **FDA regulation**: AI/ML-based Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) is regulated in the US. The FDA's Pre-Determined Change Control Plan allows adaptive AI that retrains on new data, but any change to intended use requires re-submission.

    - **Clinical validation**: performance on a research benchmark does not equal clinical utility. Randomised controlled trials (or prospective observational studies) measure whether deploying an AI system actually improves patient outcomes, not just diagnostic accuracy.

    - **Privacy**: clinical data is highly sensitive. Federated learning (train models on distributed hospital data without centralising it) and differential privacy (add calibrated noise to protect individual records) are the dominant privacy-preserving approaches.
